Where Control Lives in an AI System

A framework with six bands, two channels, and what escapes

David J. Carlson, with Claude as contributor  ·  Version 2  ·  16 October 2026

A deployed AI system is not a model. It is six bands — weights, serving, gateway, inference, wrapper, product — each with its own owner and its own way of failing, flanked by two channel boundaries (content and action) that bound what it can output and what it can do.

What the bands are for. None was built as a safety architecture. A checkpoint, the weights at a point in time, answers nothing on its own: no endpoint, no account of who is asking, no memory of the request, no tools, no knowledge of anything outside its training data. The serving band makes it compute; the gateway serves it at scale; the inference band turns a general model into one that does a particular job, with a prompt that frames the task, tools that let it act and retrieval that lets it use what an organization knows; the wrapper carries work across many steps instead of one turn; the product band makes it usable by a specialist, with connectors to real data sources and provenance on every result. Agents calling agents is what the assembled stack makes possible.

And why failing, rather than doing. Those same structures are what stands between the world and the information encoded in a checkpoint, which is enough to harm individuals, societies, or humankind. Containment is a second job asked of structures built for a first, which is why each band is described here by how it fails: so a failure can be caught in time to repair or mitigate it, and because a control that fails without announcing itself compensates for nothing. A control that must hold when the model is mistaken, absent or adversarial cannot be a disposition, and dispositions are all that can currently be trained into the checkpoint.

Bound the control to what, though. Not to zero, and not to one number. Very wide availability of conventional-explosive chemistry is tolerated everywhere; almost none is tolerated for the pathogen classes under nucleic-acid screening. Not different in kind — different in how many people one successful use of that information can reach and whether the harm stops on its own. And where the line goes is not a technical determination. A model may be trained on restricted data, or trained in ways that give it tendencies, but nothing in the model currently decides what it should be prevented from doing. Whoever is positioned to decide does, for reasons running from irreversible harm through law, liability and product tone to a government’s decisions about what may be said. The former bounds misuse, the latter bounds what citizens may be told, and both configure the same six bands. That input this framework does not supply.

Which brings the weights into focus. Every control trained into the weights is a disposition rather than a constraint. The weights band does not currently hold a constraint in the sense used here — something that binds when the model is mistaken, absent, or adversarial. Which is why the useful questions are which of the other five bands the control lives in, who owns that band, and how it fails.

The two channels

The content channel can have a control at every band. The action channel has none at the weights or the serving band — the dotted segment on the right. Nothing that a model is trained to be constrains what it is able to do; refusal training makes a model reluctant to choose an action, and does nothing to its reach. An activation monitor in the serving band differs in kind but not in channel: it does not depend on the model’s choice, and what it flags can stop a response, including one that carries a tool call, but it does not change what any response could reach. The controls on that channel are called the affordance gate — tool definitions, hooks and permission rules, in that order; the operating-system sandbox that binds execution sits outside the stack and backstops the gate rather than belonging to it.

Why the gateway band matters more each year

A growing share of capable models ships as weights alone. A published checkpoint arrives with no gateway band at all, not a weak one but none, and whoever downloads it owns every band above.

So for an open-weight deployment the only band that ships is the one whose controls are dispositions. Everything that still holds when the model is mistaken has to be assembled above it afterward, by whoever assembles it — a different question from how the model was trained, and answerable only by naming the bands actually present.

The thinness cuts both ways, which is worth saying plainly. It is what makes a published checkpoint hard to contain. It is also what makes it hard to conscript: the same weakness that defeats a safety disposition defeats an imposed account of what may be said.

The method

Six questions that outlast the answers

Asked of any band, and of any control inside one. They can be asked of a system this framework has never heard of. Select any band above to see all six answered for it.

  • Who owns itWho answers for defects and decides what ships.
  • Who can change itAfter deployment — and whether anyone is told.
  • Who can observe itAnd from which of the three positions.
  • What it can reachThe action channel, and the affordance gate that bounds it.
  • How it failsLoudly, or without anything marking the moment.
  • Whether it still binds at hour fortyTime-invariant, or decaying as the run lengthens.

Version 2 · 16 October 2026

What changed in Version 2

Version 2 adds a sixth band. The first edition, dated 11 September 2026, described five: weights, gateway, inference, wrapper and product. It placed the software that serves a checkpoint outside all of them, on the ground that it held no controls of its own. That no longer holds. Operators now place controls there on their own authority: activation monitors that read a running model’s internal state, sampling changes such as watermarking, and cached computation the caller does not control. Version 2 sets these in a serving band, owned by the operator, between the weights and the gateway. The central claim is unchanged: a control that must hold when the model is mistaken, absent or adversarial cannot be a disposition.

Four other changes run through the document:

  1. —
    Change notice.The first edition said gateway changes reach deployments without anyone being told. Version 2 says notice of gateway and serving changes is at the owner’s discretion. Some developers and operators announce changes, and nothing requires them to.
  2. —
    Content-channel layers.The content channel still has five layers in every served deployment. Version 2 adds a sixth, an activation monitor in the serving band, which is present only where an operator runs one, and so is not counted among the five.
  3. —
    Subsection numbers.Subsections are now numbered (4.2, C.1) so passages can be cited precisely. Please cite this edition as Version 2.
  4. —
    New material.A model that sets up and runs copies of itself, as the extreme case for the serving band; why a model’s knowledge needs more containing than the same knowledge in a person; the framework’s adversaries in one list; and several new definitions and comparisons with current practice.

Limits

What this framework cannot do

Stated because a decomposition is more useful when it names where it stops than when it appears to work everywhere.

  1. —
    No process dimension.Nothing says when in a lifecycle a band is built, reviewed or certified — which is what a conformity regime needs to act on any of it.
  2. —
    No capability gradation.The framework cannot yet say that at some capability level a particular band becomes mandatory.
  3. —
    Multi-agent structure, mostly not.Two claims about a single edge. Branching, cycles, error amplification across hops and agents sampling one model are all outside it. A companion paper extends the framework to spawning and to agents that reach one another, and marks each place it goes beyond what the framework establishes.
  4. —
    No magnitude for duration.The sixth question sorts controls into what holds and what decays, but supplies a direction and not a rate.
  5. —
    No numbered tiers, deliberately.Premature quantification is how the most successful levels framework in engineering acquired a tier that functions mainly as marketing.
